The Four Migration Approaches and What They Cost

Cloud migration is not a single activity. It is a spectrum of approaches, each with materially different cost, timeline and risk profiles. The approach you choose determines more of the total cost than any other single factor.

Lift and shift (Rehost)

Applications are moved to the cloud with minimal changes. Fastest to execute. Lowest professional services cost. Does not capture the full efficiency benefits of cloud-native architecture and can result in higher ongoing cloud bills than re-platforming or re-architecting. Best for legacy applications that need to exit a data centre quickly or for applications with limited remaining lifespan.

Re-platform

Applications are moved to the cloud with targeted optimisations — containerisation, managed database services, auto-scaling configuration — without a full code rewrite. Balances speed with cloud efficiency. The most common approach for mid-size enterprise migrations where cost-benefit analysis justifies the additional work over pure lift-and-shift.

Re-architect (Cloud-native)

Applications are redesigned from the ground up to use cloud-native services — microservices, serverless functions, managed data services, event-driven architecture. Highest professional services cost. Delivers the strongest long-term cloud economics and operational resilience. Required for high-traffic applications where scalability and cost efficiency at scale are critical.

Retire and replace

Legacy applications are decommissioned and replaced with SaaS alternatives or modern replacements rather than migrated. Often the most cost-efficient outcome for applications with commodity functionality. Requires a thorough application portfolio assessment to identify candidates.

The Hidden Costs Most Estimates Miss

This is where most cloud migration budgets fail. The professional services cost is visible and quoted upfront. The costs below are frequently absent from initial estimates and surface during or after migration execution.

Network egress fees

Moving data out of a cloud provider's network incurs egress charges. For enterprises with large data volumes — databases, file stores, backup repositories — egress costs during initial data transfer can add $15,000 to $80,000 to a mid-market migration that was not budgeted for this line item. Ongoing egress costs for cross-region or cross-cloud data movement also represent a recurring cost that does not appear in pre-migration estimates.

Application refactoring discovered during migration

A thorough pre-migration assessment surfaces most refactoring requirements. An inadequate assessment misses them. When unexpected refactoring requirements emerge during execution — hardcoded IP addresses, unsupported OS versions, tightly coupled dependencies — timeline extends and cost increases. Budget a 15 to 25 percent contingency reserve for application refactoring on any migration with more than 20 applications.

Dual-running costs

During migration, most enterprises run both the legacy and cloud environment simultaneously to validate functionality and ensure rollback capability. This dual-running period — typically 4 to 12 weeks — incurs both the old infrastructure cost and the new cloud cost simultaneously. For mid-market enterprises this can add $20,000 to $60,000 to the total migration cost.

Cloud operations upskilling

Your internal team needs to operate the cloud environment after migration. Cloud operations training, certification programmes and the productivity dip during the learning curve represent a real cost that rarely appears in migration estimates. Budget $5,000 to $20,000 per engineer for structured cloud training programmes depending on role and depth of skill required.

FinOps and cost optimisation

Enterprises that migrate to the cloud without establishing FinOps governance from day one frequently find their cloud bills 30 to 50 percent higher than projected in the first year. Right-sizing, reserved instance purchasing, spot instance strategies and idle resource elimination require dedicated attention during the first 90 days post-migration to establish cost efficiency baselines.

Complete cost checklist for your migration estimate

  • Professional services — architecture, engineering, project management, QA
  • Application assessment and discovery tooling costs
  • Network egress fees for initial data transfer
  • Refactoring contingency reserve — 15 to 25 percent of services cost
  • Dual-running infrastructure cost during migration window
  • Cloud operations training and certification for internal team
  • FinOps tooling and governance setup in first 90 days post-migration
  • Security architecture review and compliance validation
  • Ongoing cloud management cost if no internal capability exists

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does cloud migration cost in 2026?

Cloud migration costs vary widely. A lift-and-shift for a mid-size enterprise with 20 to 50 applications costs $80,000 to $250,000 in professional services. Re-platforming costs $150,000 to $500,000. Full re-architecture of a complex enterprise system costs $500,000 to $2 million or more. India-based delivery partners reduce professional services cost by 50 to 65 percent while delivering equivalent quality.

What are the hidden costs of cloud migration?

The most common hidden costs include network egress fees during data transfer, application refactoring discovered during migration that was not visible in the assessment, dual-running infrastructure costs during the migration window, cloud operations upskilling for your internal team, and FinOps governance setup to prevent runaway cloud bills in the first year post-migration.

Which cloud provider is cheapest for migration in 2026 — AWS, Azure or GCP?

There is no single cheapest provider. AWS has the broadest service catalogue and most mature migration tooling. Azure is the natural choice for Microsoft-heavy enterprises with existing EA agreements. GCP offers the most competitive pricing for data-heavy and AI workloads and has the most favourable egress pricing. The best total cost outcome comes from mapping specific workloads to the most cost-efficient provider rather than defaulting to a single cloud.
