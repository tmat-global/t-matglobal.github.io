DevOps Managed Services: Every Question Engineering Leaders Ask — Answered

Direct answers to the most common questions about DevOps managed services — what is included, what it costs, how SLAs work, how India-based teams handle time zones, what certifications matter, and how to evaluate providers.

By T-Mat Global Published April 17, 2026 7 min read

Engineering leaders evaluating managed DevOps have a consistent set of questions. The answers below are direct — no sales framing, no hedging on the difficult questions about costs, SLAs, and time zones.

What do DevOps managed services include?

DevOps managed services include CI/CD pipeline management, Infrastructure as Code maintenance using Terraform, Kubernetes cluster operations, observability stack management with Prometheus and Grafana, cloud cost governance, and security operations including vulnerability scanning and secrets management. The specific scope is defined in an SLA at engagement start — a credible provider will itemize exactly what is covered before you sign.

How much do DevOps managed services cost per month?

From India: $1,500–$2,500 per month for a part-time support retainer. $5,000–$12,000 per month for a full managed DevOps team. $7,000–$18,000 per month for combined DevOps and security operations. US-based equivalents cost 3–4x more for the same scope. T-Mat Global's managed DevOps retainers start at $2,800 per month for dedicated part-time coverage.

What is the difference between DevOps consulting and DevOps managed services?

DevOps consulting is project-based — the consultant implements a capability and then disengages. DevOps managed services is ongoing — the team handles day-to-day operations on a retainer basis indefinitely. Consulting builds the capability; managed services operates it. Most mature engineering organisations use consulting to implement their DevOps stack and then transition to a managed services retainer for ongoing operations support.

What SLA should I expect from a managed DevOps provider?

A credible provider should commit to: P1 (production down) response within 15–30 minutes, P2 (degraded performance) within 1–2 hours, P3 (non-urgent) within 4–8 hours during business hours. The SLA should name specific engineers assigned to your account, define escalation paths, specify coverage hours, and include monthly reporting deliverables. Any provider that resists SLA commitments should be disqualified.

How do India-based DevOps teams handle time zone differences?

India (IST, UTC+5:30) overlaps with UAE (GST, UTC+4) by nearly the full business day — only 1.5 hours apart. UK overlap is 4.5–5.5 hours, enabling afternoon coverage. US East Coast overlap is 3–4 hours in early morning IST. T-Mat Global commits to specific daily overlap windows in all managed services contracts and uses async-first tooling (Slack, Jira, GitHub) for communication outside overlap hours.

What certifications should a managed DevOps team hold?

At minimum: AWS Certified DevOps Engineer Professional (or Azure DevOps Engineer Expert, or GCP Professional DevOps Engineer depending on your cloud). Kubernetes certification (CKA or CKAD) for teams managing containerized workloads. Verify certifications at the team level — not just the individual presented during sales — and confirm the certified engineers will work on your engagement, not just exist in the company headcount.

What should a managed DevOps monthly report include?

A monthly managed DevOps report should include: deployment frequency and change failure rate (DORA metrics), cloud cost versus previous month with optimization actions taken, incident count and mean time to recovery, pipeline uptime and build success rate, security scan results and resolved vulnerabilities, and a forward-looking actions list for the next month. Reports that contain only narrative without metrics are not useful for tracking provider performance.
