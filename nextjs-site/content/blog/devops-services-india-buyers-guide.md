More companies than ever are looking at India for DevOps, cloud and platform engineering support, whether to add capacity, cover more hours or bring in specialist skills. The market is large and uneven. Providers range from global IT services firms to small specialist teams and individual consultants, and the quality of what you receive depends far more on the people, the process and the contract than on the label.

This guide is written for the people who have to choose: CTOs, heads of engineering, platform leads and procurement teams. It explains what DevOps services include, the types of providers, how to compare them, which security and data protection checks to make and how to start with a pilot. It does not recommend a provider, and it is not legal advice. Standards, tools and rules change, so confirm the details that matter to you.

## What are DevOps services?

DevOps services are engineering services that design, build and run the pipelines, infrastructure, security controls and operating processes that let a company release software quickly and keep it reliable.

A complete scope usually includes:

- **CI/CD and release engineering:** automated build, test and deployment pipelines, and safe release strategies.
- **Infrastructure as code:** cloud environments defined, reviewed and repeatable.
- **Cloud architecture and operations:** design, migration, cost control and day-to-day running on AWS, Azure or Google Cloud.
- **Containers and Kubernetes:** clusters, workloads and their operation.
- **Observability and incident response:** monitoring, alerting, on-call and post-incident learning.
- **Security and compliance engineering:** pipeline security, secrets, vulnerability management and audit evidence.
- **Resilience:** backups, recovery and disaster recovery testing.

## Why do companies choose India for DevOps?

Companies choose India for access to experienced engineers, flexible capacity and time zone coverage, and they should compare total cost, not hourly rates alone.

- **Skills and depth:** a large pool of engineers with cloud, Kubernetes and automation experience.
- **Flexible capacity:** you can buy a project, a retainer or dedicated engineers, and change the mix.
- **Time zones:** India is 1.5 hours ahead of the UAE, 4.5 to 5.5 hours ahead of the UK depending on daylight saving, and 9.5 to 10.5 hours ahead of US Eastern time. Overlap is strong with the UAE, partial with the UK and short with the US, so US teams usually rely on written handovers and agreed on-call arrangements.
- **Cost structure:** often lower than local hiring, but include your own management time, tooling, coverage hours and the cost of rework when comparing.

## What types of DevOps providers are there?

There are five common types, and each trades scale against depth, attention and flexibility.

1. **Large IT services firms.** Broad portfolios, formal processes and large benches. They suit big, long programmes, but smaller engagements may get less senior attention and less flexibility.
2. **Specialist DevOps and cloud consultancies.** Deeper expertise in a narrower area, with more flexible engagement. The bench is smaller, so check availability.
3. **Founder-led delivery partners.** Close senior involvement and fast decisions. Capacity is smaller and track records may be shorter, so judge them through a scoped pilot.
4. **Tooling and platform vendors with services teams.** Strong on their own stack, but their advice can favour their product.
5. **Independent consultants.** Low overhead and direct access to one expert. Continuity and out-of-hours cover can be difficult.

Match the type to the need: scale, depth, continuity or speed. Many companies use a mix.

## Which engagement models are available?

The common models are an assessment, a fixed-scope project, a managed retainer, dedicated engineers and a hybrid of these.

- **Assessment:** a short, fixed-price review that ends in a prioritised plan.
- **Fixed-scope project:** a defined outcome such as a pipeline build, a migration or a cost review.
- **Managed retainer:** ongoing operations, monitoring and support with agreed response times.
- **Dedicated engineers:** the provider's engineers work as part of your team under your direction.
- **Hybrid:** an assessment, then a project, then a retainer, or an external team that builds the foundations while you hire.

Pricing usually follows the model: fixed fees for assessments and projects, monthly fees for managed services and time-based rates for dedicated engineers.

## How do you evaluate a DevOps provider in India?

Evaluate on evidence of hands-on work, delivery discipline, security practices and commercial clarity, using a scorecard you agree before you meet providers.

An example scorecard. Adjust the weights to your priorities, and score each provider from 1 to 5:

- **Technical depth in your stack (20%):** ask for specific examples of similar work and who did it.
- **People and continuity (15%):** who will actually work on your account, their experience and how knowledge is shared.
- **Delivery process (15%):** how work is planned, reviewed, documented and reported.
- **Security practices (15%):** how they secure their own devices, accounts and access to yours.
- **Data protection and compliance (10%):** their approach to your obligations and their evidence.
- **Communication and overlap (10%):** time zone overlap, handovers, escalation and on-call.
- **Ownership and exit (10%):** everything in your repositories and accounts, documentation you own and transition support.
- **Commercial terms (5%):** clear scope, exclusions and change pricing.

Ask every provider the same questions, and ask for references or, if a provider is newer, for a scoped pilot instead.

## What security, privacy and regulatory checks should you make?

Treat the provider's engineers like any new team with powerful access, and check how data protection and incident rules apply to the engagement.

**Access model**

- Single sign-on with multi-factor authentication, and named accounts with no shared logins.
- Least-privilege, time-limited access, with approvals for production changes.
- Work done in your cloud accounts and repositories, with audit logging you can review.
- A checklist to remove access the day the engagement or a person's role ends.

**Data protection and incident reporting in India**

- India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is now supported by the DPDP Rules, notified in November 2025, which phase in over 18 months, with most obligations applying from May 2027.
- The rules require affected people and the Data Protection Board to be told of a personal data breach without delay, with a detailed report to the Board within 72 hours.
- Separately, CERT-In's 2022 directions require listed cyber incidents to be reported within six hours, and logs to be kept for 180 days.
- Make sure your contract and the provider's incident process align with these clocks and with your own, so that one incident runbook works for both.

**Cross-border data and regulated sectors**

- If personal data from the UK or EU is involved, ask your legal adviser which transfer mechanism applies.
- Regulators in some sectors limit where data and systems may sit and who may access them. For example, the Central Bank of the UAE has rules for licensed financial institutions on keeping certain core records in the UAE and on approval of material outsourcing. If you are regulated, agree an access model with your compliance team and regulator before work starts. It may need UAE-based jump hosts, no data leaving approved locations and tighter logging.

**Evidence**

- Ask for the provider's own security evidence, such as ISO 27001 or SOC 2 reports, and read the scope. Do not accept a logo without a report, and a smaller provider may offer a questionnaire and a pilot instead.

## How should pricing and contracts work?

Get scope, coverage hours, response times and exclusions in writing, and compare total cost, not the headline rate.

- **Scope and deliverables,** including what is excluded and how changes are priced.
- **Service levels:** coverage hours, response times by severity and escalation contacts.
- **Ownership:** your company owns the code, infrastructure definitions, runbooks and documentation.
- **Confidentiality and data protection:** a non-disclosure agreement and a data processing agreement.
- **Security obligations:** access controls, device security and breach notification times.
- **Termination and transition:** notice, handover support and removal of access.

Be careful with quotes far below others, and ask what has been left out. Have your legal counsel review the contract.

## What does a good pilot look like?

A good pilot is a bounded, four to six week piece of real work with agreed success criteria, so that both sides can judge the fit before committing further.

- Pick a defined task, such as a pipeline improvement, a cost review, a monitoring set-up or a resilience test.
- Agree success criteria in advance, such as delivery metrics (deployment frequency, lead time, change failure rate and time to restore), response times and documentation quality.
- Use the real access model you would use later.
- Review the results together and decide on the next step.

## What does a 90-day onboarding plan look like?

- **Days 1 to 14:** agree scope, access model, contacts and communication rhythm, and review the current environment.
- **Days 15 to 45:** run the pilot or first project, with weekly reviews and documentation in your repositories.
- **Days 46 to 90:** expand to the next priorities, start on-call or support if agreed, set up reporting and review results against your criteria.

## What mistakes do buyers make?

- Choosing on hourly rate without comparing coverage, quality and your own management time.
- Giving broad access on day one, or sharing logins.
- Leaving ownership of code and documentation unclear.
- Skipping a pilot and committing to a long contract.
- Accepting certifications and logos without checking scope and dates.
- Not agreeing how incidents, handovers and time zone gaps are handled.
- Forgetting data protection and regulatory limits until after work has started.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are DevOps services?

They are engineering services that build and run the pipelines, cloud infrastructure, security controls and operations that let a company release software quickly and keep it reliable.

### Why outsource DevOps to India?

For access to experienced engineers, flexible capacity and time zone coverage. Compare total cost, including your own management time, not hourly rates alone.

### How do I choose a DevOps company in India?

Use a scorecard covering technical depth in your stack, the people who will do the work, delivery process, security practices, data protection, communication, ownership and exit terms, and test the fit with a scoped pilot.

### How much do DevOps services cost in India?

It depends on scope, coverage hours, complexity and the engagement model. Ask for a written scope with exclusions and compare response times and total cost, not only the monthly figure.

### Is it safe to give an India-based provider access to production?

It can be, with named accounts, multi-factor authentication, least-privilege and time-limited access, audit logs, approvals for production changes and everything kept in your own accounts and repositories.

### What data protection rules apply to an India-based provider?

India's DPDP Act and the DPDP Rules 2025, phased in over 18 months, plus CERT-In's incident reporting directions. If you handle UK or EU personal data, or you are in a regulated sector, other rules apply too. Ask your legal adviser.

### How long does it take to start working with a DevOps provider?

Often a few weeks to agree scope, access and a pilot. Start with an assessment or a bounded project before expanding.

### Can we start with a small engagement?

Yes, and it is the safest way to begin. An assessment or a pilot lets you judge the work before you commit to more.
